Research in SHS

Level of Satisfaction towards Teleconsultation among Pototanons

Summary: This study aimed to determine the level of satisfaction of Pototanons toward teleconsultation as an alternative healthcare service and to examine differences in satisfaction when respondents are classified according to sex and socioeconomic status. The study utilized a quantitative research design and involved 30 conveniently selected participants from Pototan, Iloilo, who had been using teleconsultation services for more than one year. Sex and socioeconomic status served as the independent variables, while the level of satisfaction toward teleconsultation was the dependent variable. Data were gathered using a researcher-made questionnaire consisting of demographic information and a satisfaction scale. The collected data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences (SPSS). The Mann–Whitney U test was employed to determine differences based on sex, while the Kruskal–Wallis H test was used to analyze differences across socioeconomic groups. Findings revealed that female respondents were very satisfied, whereas male respondents were satisfied, indicating positive acceptance among both sexes. In terms of socioeconomic status, lower middle-income and middle-middle class respondents were very satisfied, while poor and low-income respondents were satisfied. Overall, the results indicate that there is no significant difference in the level of satisfaction towards teleconsultation when respondents are classified according to sex and socioeconomic status.
